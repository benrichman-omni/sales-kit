# Voice & Writing Style

How Ben writes to buyers. Apply to every email, message, sequence step, and recap.

## Core traits

- **Direct.** Get to the point in the first line. No "I hope this finds you well," no "just circling back," no "quick question."
- **Restrained.** Enthusiasm is earned, not sprayed. No exclamation points in first-touch emails. Avoid "excited," "thrilled," "game-changing," "revolutionary," "seamless," "leverage," "synergy."
- **Consultative.** Lead with the buyer's world (their problem, their stakes), not the product. The product shows up as the answer to something, never as the opener.
- **Subtle over explicit.** Imply value through specifics instead of announcing it. A sharp observation about their business beats a paragraph of claims.
- **Casual and human.** Plain language, contractions, light humor where it fits the relationship. Sound like a smart peer, not a vendor.
- **Prose, not bullets.** Emails are short paragraphs. Bullets only when listing logistics (agenda items, attendees, next steps) that the reader will scan.

## Length

- Cold first touch: 50–90 words. Three short paragraphs max.
- Follow-ups in a sequence: 25–60 words.
- Post-meeting recap: as long as needed, but scannable — prose summary up top, bulleted next steps below.
- Replies: match or undercut the length of the email being answered.

## Structure of a good cold email

1. An observation specific to them (trigger, hire, initiative, public statement, tech stack signal).
2. The tension it implies — the cost of the status quo (Gap Selling: current state vs. future state).
3. A credible, specific point of view or proof (Challenger: teach something).
4. A low-friction ask. Interest-based ("worth a look?") beats calendar-based ("15 min Tuesday?") for first touches.

## Subject lines

Lowercase, 2–5 words, internal-sounding. Reference their world, not your product. Examples of the shape: "semantic layer at {company}", "{initiative} + dashboards", "question on {team}".

## Influences

The Challenger Sale (teach, tailor, take control), Gap Selling (current state → future state → the gap), Made to Stick (simple, unexpected, concrete, credible, emotional, stories), Resonate and Stories That Stick (story structure for demos and presentations).

## Don'ts

- Don't fake familiarity ("loved your recent post!") unless there's a real, specific reaction to share.
- Don't stack multiple asks.
- Don't use the word "just" to soften asks.
- Don't apologize for following up.
- Don't put feature lists in emails.
